## The three roles (per brief: they can differ, and they do)

- **BEST CASH ENGINE: the CRM/data service (crm-scan lists + enrichment).** Zero build cost, near-100% margin, weeks to first revenue: but it consumes selling hours forever and caps where the hours cap.
- **BEST COMPOUNDING ENGINE: the Etsy winner.** Every listing, review, pin and email subscriber persists and stacks; marginal production cost keeps falling as the engines mature; two tax seasons a year compound the flywheel.
- **BEST LONG-TERM BUSINESS: the Etsy winner graduating to its own Shopify brand.** A named UK small-business tools brand with catalogue + list + storefront is a sellable asset in a way none of the alternatives are at this scale.

## Portfolio recommendation

Run the Etsy build as the primary project (it needs the Sept-Jan season NOW), keep the crm-scan service as the optional cash layer only if hours allow (one list deal ≈ a month of early Etsy profit), and leave arbitrage/FBA/KDP/POD parked. Do not let aufentic dropshipping interleave with this: separate brand, separate hours, per the operating decision in strategy/positioning.md.

## Honesty check (brief section 38: do not oversell Etsy)

Is Etsy digital actually mediocre for this operator? The genuine weaknesses: cold-start discovery risk, platform dependence, and a median-case outcome (across all digital sellers) of under £100/mo. The recommendation survives because this plan does not buy the median: it buys a proven premium sub-category, a regulatory catalyst with dates, a differentiation set mapped to documented complaints, and a production cost per listing that lets 100+ quality listings exist inside a ≤10h/wk budget. If month-3 data (see 90-day plan kill/pivot gates) comes back LOW-case, the honest move is to halve the effort into the CRM/data cash engine: and that decision gate is written into the plan.
